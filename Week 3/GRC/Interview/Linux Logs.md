# Interview Questions

### Q1. What are Linux logs?

**Answer:**  
Linux logs are records of events and activities generated by the operating system, applications, services, and other components.

### Q2. Where are Linux logs commonly stored?

**Answer:**  
Many traditional Linux log files are stored under:

/var/log

### Q3. Why are logs important in cybersecurity?

**Answer:**  
Logs provide evidence that can help analysts **detect suspicious activity, troubleshoot problems, and investigate security incidents**.

### Q4. What is `/var/log/auth.log`?

**Answer:**  
On Ubuntu and other Debian-based systems, it commonly contains authentication and authorization-related events.

### Q5. What does `tail -f` do?

**Answer:**  
It displays the end of a file and continues showing **new entries as they are added**.

### Q6. What is `journalctl`?

**Answer:**  
`journalctl` is a command used to view logs collected by the **systemd journal**.

### Q7. Can all Linux systems have exactly the same log files?

**Answer:**  
No. Log locations and filenames can vary depending on the **Linux distribution and logging configuration**.

# Scenario-Based Question

### Q. A security analyst suspects that someone has repeatedly tried to log in to an Ubuntu server with incorrect credentials. Where could they look for evidence?

**Answer:**  
They could examine the authentication logs, commonly:

/var/log/auth.log

They could also use the systemd journal where applicable.
