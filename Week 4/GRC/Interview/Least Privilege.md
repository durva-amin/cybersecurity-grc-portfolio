# Interview Questions

### Q1. What is the Principle of Least Privilege?

**Answer:**  
Least Privilege means giving users, applications, or systems only the minimum permissions required to perform their tasks.

### Q2. Why is Least Privilege important?

**Answer:**  
It reduces unauthorized access and limits the potential damage caused by compromised accounts, mistakes, or misuse.

### Q3. Give an example of Least Privilege.

**Answer:**  
A normal employee receives access to the files required for their work but does not receive administrator privileges.

### Q4. Should every employee have administrator access?

**Answer:**  
No. Administrator access should only be provided when required for the person's responsibilities.

### Q5. How does Least Privilege help if an account is compromised?

**Answer:**  
The attacker is limited to the permissions available to that account, which can reduce the amount of damage they can cause.

### Q6. How can an organization maintain Least Privilege?

**Answer:**  
By granting access based on job requirements, regularly reviewing permissions, and removing unnecessary or outdated access.

### Q7. What should happen when an employee changes roles?

**Answer:**  
Their access should be reviewed. Permissions that are no longer required should be removed, and only necessary new permissions should be granted.

# Scenario-Based Question

### Q. During an access review, you discover that a marketing employee has administrator access even though their job doesn't require it. What would you recommend?

**Answer:**  
I would recommend reviewing the employee's access requirements and **removing unnecessary administrator privileges**.

They should receive only the permissions necessary for their marketing responsibilities.

This follows the **Principle of Least Privilege**.
