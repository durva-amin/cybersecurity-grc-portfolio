# Interview Questions

### Q1. What is a Compensating Control?

**Answer:**  
A compensating control is an alternative security control used when the preferred or required control cannot be implemented, while still reducing the associated risk.

### Q2. Give an example of a Compensating Control.

**Answer:**  
If a legacy application cannot support MFA, an organization might restrict access to trusted networks and increase monitoring as compensating controls.

### Q3. Why are Compensating Controls needed?

**Answer:**  
Because sometimes the required control cannot be implemented due to technical, business, cost, or operational limitations.

### Q4. Can an organization simply skip a required control?

**Answer:**  
Not automatically. The organization should assess the risk and applicable requirements, determine whether an appropriate compensating control is acceptable, and document and approve the decision.

### Q5. Are Compensating Controls temporary?

**Answer:**  
They can be temporary or longer-term depending on the situation and applicable requirements, but they should be regularly reviewed to ensure they remain effective.

### Q6. What should a GRC professional check for a Compensating Control?

**Answer:**  
They should check the reason for using it, associated risk, effectiveness of the alternative control, documentation, approval, and review process.

# Scenario-Based Question

### Q. During an audit, you discover that an old application cannot support MFA. What would you recommend?

**Answer:**  
I would first identify and assess the risk. If MFA cannot currently be implemented, I would recommend suitable **compensating controls**, such as restricted access and additional monitoring, and ensure the exception and controls are properly documented, approved, and reviewed.
