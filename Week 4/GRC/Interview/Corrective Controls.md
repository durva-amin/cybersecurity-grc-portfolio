# Interview Questions

### Q1. What is a Corrective Control?

**Answer:**  
A corrective control is a security measure used to fix problems, reduce their impact, and restore systems after an incident has occurred or been detected.

### Q2. Give examples of Corrective Controls.

**Answer:**  
Restoring from backups, removing malware, resetting compromised passwords, applying patches, and rebuilding affected systems.

### Q3. Is backup a Corrective Control?

**Answer:**  
The **backup itself** supports recovery, while **restoring data from a backup** is a clear example of a corrective action.

### Q4. What is the difference between Detective and Corrective Controls?

**Answer:**  
A detective control **identifies the problem**, while a corrective control helps **fix or recover from the problem**.

### Q5. Can patching be a Corrective Control?

**Answer:**  
Yes. If a vulnerability has been identified and a patch is applied to correct it, patching can act as a corrective control. Depending on context, patching can also have a preventive purpose against future exploitation.

### Q6. Why are Corrective Controls important?

**Answer:**  
Because not every security incident can be prevented. Corrective controls help organizations recover and reduce the damage caused by incidents.

# Scenario-Based Question

### Q. An attacker compromises an employee account. The security team detects it, disables the session, resets the password, and restores affected files. Which actions are Corrective Controls?

**Answer:**  
The **password reset and restoration of affected files** are corrective actions because they help fix the problem and recover from the incident.
