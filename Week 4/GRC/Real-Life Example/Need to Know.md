## Real-Life Example

Suppose a company is investigating an employee complaint.

Only people directly involved, such as authorized **HR personnel**, may need access to the investigation documents.

Other employees do not need that information.

Therefore:

**Authorized HR → Access ✅**

**Unrelated employees → No access ❌**

This follows the **Need-to-Know principle**.

---

## Least Privilege vs Need to Know

This is an important interview distinction.

|Principle|Focus|Question|
|---|---|---|
|**Least Privilege**|Minimum permissions|**What can you do?**|
|**Need to Know**|Necessary information|**What do you need to see/know?**|

### Example

An employee may have permission to access a shared HR system, but they should only see the employee records necessary for their work.

> **Least Privilege → Minimum access/permissions**  
> **Need to Know → Minimum necessary information**
