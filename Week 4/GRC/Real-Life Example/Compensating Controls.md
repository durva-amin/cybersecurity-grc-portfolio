## Real-Life Example

Suppose an old application **does not support MFA**.

The organization cannot immediately replace the application.

Instead, it may implement:

- Restricted network access
- Stronger access restrictions
- Increased login monitoring
- Additional approval requirements

These can act as **compensating controls** while the original MFA requirement cannot be implemented.

> The alternative should actually reduce the relevant risk; simply choosing an easier control is not enough.

---

## Important GRC Point

In GRC, you may encounter:

**Required Control → Cannot Implement → Assess Risk → Select Compensating Control → Document & Approve → Monitor**

Documentation should explain:

- Why the original control cannot be implemented
- What risk exists
- What alternative control is being used
- How the alternative reduces the risk
- Who approved it
- When it should be reviewed
